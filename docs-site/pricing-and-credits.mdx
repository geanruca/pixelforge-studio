---
title: 'Pricing and credits'
description: 'Why the price is a range, what is reserved, and what you are charged.'
---

Credits are US dollars. An image costs what the image service bills for it plus a 25% markup, and nothing is rounded to cents: a typical image costs between one and ten cents, so rounding each one would distort its price.

## The price is a range until the image is done

Part of the cost depends on how many tokens the model ends up using, which is only known on completion. Before that you get three numbers:

| Field                | Meaning                                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `minimum_price_usd`  | The least it can cost.                                       |
| `expected_price_usd` | The most likely price. This is the estimate shown to people. |
| `maximum_price_usd`  | The most it can cost. **This is what gets reserved.**        |

## Reserve, then charge

| When                    | What happens to your credits                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| You create a generation | `maximum_price_usd` is reserved. If your balance does not cover it, nothing is created and you get `402 insufficient_credits`. |
| It succeeds             | You are charged `price_usd`, the actual price. The rest of the reservation returns to your balance.                            |
| It fails                | The whole reservation returns. **A failed generation costs nothing.**                                                          |

Reserving the maximum means your balance can never go negative, however many generations you have in flight.

## Reading money

Amounts are exact decimal strings with nine decimals (`"0.070625000"`). Parse them with a decimal type (`Decimal` in Python, `BigDecimal` in Java, `bcmath` in PHP). Do not parse them as floats: `0.1 + 0.2` is not `0.3` in floating point.

`GET /balance` returns the credits available to spend. Amounts reserved for generations in progress are not included.
